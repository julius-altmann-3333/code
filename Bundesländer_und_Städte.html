German Cities by Bundesland

Baden-Württemberg

Stuttgart

Capital city of Baden-Württemberg. Known for its automotive industry, home to Mercedes-Benz and Porsche.

Mannheim

A major industrial city, located at the junction of the Rhine and Neckar rivers.

Karlsruhe

Known for its grand palace and being the seat of Germany’s Federal Court.

Freiburg im Breisgau

Famous for its medieval architecture, and one of the greenest cities in Germany.

Heidelberg

Known for its historical university and scenic castle overlooking the Neckar River.

Bavaria (Bayern)

Munich (München)

Capital of Bavaria, famous for its beer gardens, Oktoberfest, and a rich cultural history.

Nuremberg (Nürnberg)

Known for its historical landmarks, including Nuremberg Castle and its Christmas market.

Augsburg

One of Germany’s oldest cities, known for its medieval architecture and cultural history.

Regensburg

A UNESCO World Heritage site, known for its well-preserved medieval city center.

Würzburg

Famous for the Würzburg Residence, a baroque palace and UNESCO World Heritage site.

Brandenburg

Potsdam

Capital of Brandenburg, famous for its palaces, parks, and the Brandenburg Gate of Potsdam.

Cottbus

Known for its history and as a university city, Cottbus is one of the key cities in Lusatia.

Brandenburg an der Havel

Historic town with a rich medieval background, featuring a variety of gothic and baroque architecture.

Frankfurt (Oder)

A border city on the Oder River, known for its historical university and proximity to Poland.

Oranienburg

Famous for the Sachsenhausen concentration camp memorial and its beautiful castle.

Bremen

Bremen

One of the smallest federal states, known for its historical city center and the Bremen Town Musicians fairy tale.

Bremerhaven

Located on the North Sea coast, famous for its harbor and the German Maritime Museum.

Hamburg

Hamburg

The second-largest city in Germany, famous for its port, Speicherstadt, and vibrant cultural scene.

Hesse (Hessen)

Frankfurt am Main

Germany’s financial hub, famous for its skyscrapers, museums, and historic Römer square.

Wiesbaden

Known for its spa culture, Wiesbaden is one of Germany’s oldest and most beautiful spa towns.

Darmstadt

Famous for its role in science, especially in the field of information technology and computer science.

Offenbach am Main

A city in the Frankfurt metropolitan area, known for its leather industry and museums.

Kassel

Home to the world-famous documenta contemporary art exhibition and the beautiful Wilhelmshöhe Palace.

Lower Saxony (Niedersachsen)

Hanover (Hannover)

Known for its trade fairs, beautiful parks, and the Hannover Zoo, one of the oldest zoos in the world.

Braunschweig

Known for its medieval heritage and as the former residence of the Dukes of Brunswick.

Oldenburg

Oldenburg is known for its history as a royal residence and its cultural institutions.

Osnabrück

A historic city, famous for the Peace of Westphalia, which ended the Thirty Years' War in 1648.

Wolfsburg

Known as the headquarters of Volkswagen and for the Autostadt, a large automotive-themed park.

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

Rostock

Known for its harbor, Rostock is one of the oldest cities in northern Germany with a rich maritime history.

Schwerin

The capital of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Schwerin is famous for its beautiful palace, Schwerin Castle, by the lake.

Neubrandenburg

Known for its historical old town and the scenic Neubrandenburger See.

Stralsund

A UNESCO World Heritage site with its well-preserved medieval center and connection to the Baltic Sea.

Greifswald

Famous for its university, which was founded in 1456, and its picturesque Old Town.

North Rhine-Westphalia (Nordrhein-Westfalen)

Cologne (Köln)

Famous for its stunning cathedral, Cologne is one of Germany’s oldest cities with a rich cultural scene.

Düsseldorf

Known for its fashion and trade fairs, Düsseldorf also boasts a rich art scene and is a hub for international business.

Dortmund

A major city in the Ruhr area, Dortmund is known for its football club, Dortmund BVB, and its industrial heritage.

Essen

Part of the Ruhr metropolitan region, Essen is known for its role in the coal and steel industry and its cultural institutions.

Bochum

Another city in the Ruhr area, Bochum is known for its industrial past and the Ruhr University.

Rhineland-Palatinate (Rheinland-Pfalz)

Mainz

The capital of Rhineland-Palatinate, Mainz is known for its historic old town and as the home of the Gutenberg Printing Press.

Koblenz

Located at the confluence of the Rhine and Moselle rivers, Koblenz is famous for its Deutsches Eck (German Corner) monument.

Ludwigshafen am Rhein

A key industrial city, Ludwigshafen is home to the headquarters of BASF, one of the world's largest chemical companies.

Trier

One of Germany's oldest cities, known for its well-preserved Roman monuments such as the Porta Nigra and the Trier Cathedral.

Worms

Famous for its historical significance, including being the site of the Diet of Worms in 1521 during the Reformation.

Saarland

Saarbrücken

The capital of Saarland, Saarbrücken is a key city on the Saar River and has a strong historical and cultural heritage.

Neunkirchen

Located in the south of Saarland, Neunkirchen is known for its steel industry and historic landmarks.

Sankt Wendel

Known for its beautiful surrounding nature and its historical significance as a medieval town.

Völklingen

A UNESCO World Heritage site, Völklingen is known for its former ironworks that are now a museum.

Saarlouis

Founded by the French in the 17th century, Saarlouis has a rich military history and beautiful baroque architecture.

Sachsen (Saxony)

Dresden

Known for its baroque architecture and art treasures, Dresden is often referred to as the "Florence of the Elbe."

Leipzig

A cultural hub, Leipzig is known for its music scene, vibrant arts, and being the home of the famous composer Johann Sebastian Bach.

Chemnitz

Once an industrial city, Chemnitz has transformed into a cultural center with a mix of old and new architecture.

Zwickau

Known for its automotive industry (specifically the Trabant) and beautiful old town.

Plauen

A historic town with a rich tradition in lace-making and textiles, as well as beautiful surrounding nature.
