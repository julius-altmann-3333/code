Bricklaying Robots - Where to Buy and Costs

SAM100 (Semi-Automated Mason)

Manufacturer: Construction Robotics

Where to Buy: Available directly from the Construction Robotics website or through authorized distributors.

Cost: Around $500,000 - $700,000 depending on configuration. This price includes the robot itself, but additional costs can come from installation, training, and support.

Features:

  • Semi-automated, works alongside human labor.
  • The robot lays bricks, while a human worker applies mortar and does finishing work.
  • Ideal for commercial or larger-scale construction projects.

Hadrian X (Fully Autonomous Robot)

Manufacturer: Fastbrick Robotics

Where to Buy: Fastbrick Robotics (currently under development and demonstrated in Australia).

Cost: $1.5 million - $3 million depending on scale and configuration. Primarily for large-scale construction companies.

Features:

  • Fully autonomous, capable of 3D printing bricklaying.
  • Can construct walls quickly with minimal human intervention, with plans for future mass production.

Buildbot (Open-Source Bricklaying Robot)

Manufacturer: Open-source community and academic groups

Where to Buy: Available as an open-source project, with parts or complete kits from platforms like GitHub and academic partnerships.

Cost: Typically around $50,000 - $150,000 for smaller or prototype systems.

Features:

  • Open-source and customizable, mostly used by research groups or smaller construction companies interested in experimenting with robotic bricklaying.

Volumetric Construction Robotics

Manufacturer: Volumetric Construction Robotics (UK-based)

Where to Buy: Contact directly via their website for quotes and partnerships.

Cost: Cost is currently undisclosed but expected to be around $500,000 - $1 million for the full system.

Features:

  • Includes autonomous bricklaying with mortar application.
  • Targets residential and commercial building projects.

Other Robotics Companies

Manufacturer: Various startups and robotics companies.

Where to Buy: Varies by the manufacturer and model. Some companies still have prototypes or early-stage commercialization.

Cost: Varies significantly, generally falling in the range of $500,000 - $1.5 million.

Features:

  • Includes companies like Apis Cor and Contour Crafting, focusing on 3D printing and robotic construction techniques.

Considerations for Buying Bricklaying Robots

  • Maintenance: Robot systems typically require ongoing maintenance, which can add to the long-term cost.
  • Training: Workers will need to be trained to operate and oversee these systems, which may come with additional expenses.
  • Material Supply & Site Prep: Automation works best in environments where the material supply chain is streamlined, and the site is properly prepared for robotic construction.
  • Project Scale: Many of these systems are optimized for larger-scale commercial construction projects, so their use in residential projects might be cost-prohibitive unless specifically designed for that.
